O periódico **GigaScience** publicou um estudo de alto impacto centrado em inovação computacional e ciências ômicas: *"M-SCAN: Multi-Scale Constrained Analysis of Metabolic Networks for Genome-Scale Flux Inference in Pan-Cancer Cohorts"*, por Lim S, Baek JW, Seo Y, Park N, Park SY, et al..

### O Desafio e o Contexto Computacional
BACKGROUND: Cancer metabolism reflects both recurrent tumour programmes and tissue-specific constraints, but transcriptome analyses do not directly estimate metabolic reaction fluxes. We developed Multi-Scale Constrained Analysis of Metabolic Networks (M-SCAN), a reproducible workflow for sample-wise genome-scale flux inference across heterogeneous cancer cohorts. RESULTS: M-SCAN retains the Human1 genome-scale metabolic network as a shared reaction backbone and introduces sample specificity through RNA expression, deleterious mutation effects, predicted kcat, direction-specific treatment of reversible reactions, and tissue-specific nutrient constraints. We benchmarked M-SCAN using measured exchange fluxes from 11 cancer cell lines and against an independent 13C-MFA dataset containing intracellular flux. Predicted and measured exchange fluxes were significantly correlated (Spearman ρ = 0.704, P < 0.001), and M-SCAN outperformed an enzyme constrained baseline in every cell line. We then applied M-SCAN to 7,562 tumour and normal samples from The Cancer Genome Atlas and the Genotype-Tissue Expression project across 14 cancer types. The flux profiles preserved tissue-specific metabolic structure, distinguished tumour from normal samples, and recovered flux programmes associated with clinical outcome and molecular subtype. Machine-learning analyses further identified recurrent tumour-associated pathways and stratified reactions into global-core, regional, and cancer-specific layers. CONCLUSIONS: M-SCAN provides a scalable and reproducible framework for integrating molecular, enzymatic, and environmental constraints into comparable sample-specific flux states. This framework complements RNA-based analyses by providing reaction-level flux representations for cohort-scale metabolic interpretation and hypothesis generation.
