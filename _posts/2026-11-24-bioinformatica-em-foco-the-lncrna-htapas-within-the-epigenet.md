O periódico **BMC genomics** publicou um estudo de alto impacto centrado em inovação computacional e ciências ômicas: *"The lncRNA hTAPAS within the epigenetic landscape of hTERT transcription and splicing"*, por Patterson VSJ, Limbach AG, Dogruyol JA, Esposito CA, Krämer C, et al..

### O Desafio e o Contexto Computacional
BACKGROUND: Telomerase activity is primarily controlled by expression of the catalytic subunit hTERT, yet the mechanisms coordinating its transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulation remain incompletely understood. Paradoxically, hypermethylation of a CpG-rich region upstream of the hTERT transcription start site, termed the TERT Hypermethylated Oncological Region (THOR), is strongly associated with hTERT activation. Recent evidence suggests that THOR methylation influences the expression of hTAPAS, a long non-coding RNA transcribed antisense to the hTERT promoter that represses hTERT expression. Here, we investigated the relationship between DNA methylation and alternative splicing of hTERT. RESULTS: Using CRISPR-Cas9-mediated targeted enrichment combined with Nanopore sequencing, we generated high-resolution DNA methylation maps across several kilobases surrounding the hTERT promoter and intronic regions encompassing exons 6-8 in multiple human cell lines. These analyses identified distinct methylation signatures associated with specific hTERT splice isoform profiles. Functional perturbation experiments further demonstrated that altering the epigenetic state of the hTERT locus influences alternative splicing. Overexpression of the antisense long non-coding RNA hTAPAS, as well as treatment with the DNA demethylating agent 5'-azacytidine, reduced CpG methylation at the hTERT promoter and altered the distribution of hTERT splice isoforms. These findings indicate an association among hTAPAS expression, DNA methylation at the hTERT locus, and hTERT splice isoform profiles. CONCLUSIONS: Our results identify an association between DNA methylation patterns at the hTERT locus and alternative hTERT splicing and further implicate hTAPAS expression in this regulatory context. These results support an epigenetic mechanism linking hTAPAS to hTERT promoter methylation and splice isoform selection, providing new insight into how telomerase regulation may be remodeled during development and cancer.
